The story behind the song

A song caught between feeling and restraint.

From the 2019 Bengali film Sweater, “Preme Pora Baron” is sung by Lagnajita Chakraborty and written and composed by Ranajoy Bhattacharjee. Even its title holds a contradiction: the heart may fall in love, while the world seems to say it should not.

The song belongs to Tuku’s story. As she learns to knit and begins to find confidence in herself, the film follows a young woman discovering more of who she can be. The song sits within that journey, where affection, expectation and self-discovery meet.

That is where the song finds its particular tenderness. There is warmth in the feeling, but also a hesitation around expressing it. The result is a romantic melody with a wistful edge — intimate, gentle and unmistakably part of its film’s world.

The musical identity

A melody made to carry a feeling.

“Preme Pora Baron” is a contemporary Bengali romantic film song, led by Lagnajita Chakraborty’s voice. Its melody sits at the centre of the arrangement, giving the song its gentle, memorable character.

There is already a connection to strings in the original recording: Rohan Roy is credited on violin, and Raja Chowdhury on guitar. Ranajoy Bhattacharjee is credited for the composition, lyrics, arrangement, programming and music production. In this interpretation, the melody comes forward once more — this time with the violin carrying the lead.

Listen for the way the tune moves: how it rises, settles and returns. Beyond the words, the melody gives the song its shape — and leaves room for each listener to find a feeling of their own.

The sweetness, and the hesitation.

There is tenderness in this song, but it is not entirely carefree. Its title brings a note of restraint into the romance, leaving the melody warm on the surface and wistful underneath.
